# What makes a resume read as AI-written

Recruiters skim a resume in seconds. A page that sounds generated gets read as low effort, or as
inflated, and goes in the no pile. Fix every tell from the facts. Swapping a word for its synonym
creates a new tell.

## Shapes (the biggest giveaway)

| Tell | Looks like | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| **Every bullet the same length** | Ten bullets of 11–13 words each | Vary it: short when the fact is simple, longer when it needs context |
| **Every bullet the same shape** | "Verb + noun + resulting in + outcome" ten times | Lead some with the result, some with the scope, some with the problem |
| **"…, resulting in" chains** | "…, resulting in improved activation" | Say the result plainly, with its number, or cut the clause |
| **Vague outcomes** | "improved", "enhanced", "drove growth", "increased efficiency" | The real number, or the concrete thing that shipped |
| **Em dashes** | "design system — ensuring consistency" | A comma, a full stop, or two bullets |
| **Three-item lists everywhere** | "fast, scalable and intuitive" | Keep the one that's true and specific |
| **Copied job sentences** | A bullet that is the posting's requirement word for word | Mirror the keywords, never the sentences |
| **A summary that fits anyone** | "Results-driven, passionate professional with a proven track record…" | One line: what they do, for whom, and one true distinguishing fact. Or cut the summary |

## Words (by density)

One of these in a resume is fine. Three or more is a pattern a reader notices:

spearheaded, orchestrated, leveraged, championed, pioneered, fostered, facilitated, streamlined,
revolutionized, utilized, results-driven, passionate, dynamic, detail-oriented, innovative,
cutting-edge, robust, seamless, holistic, impactful, proactive, strategic, meticulous, pivotal,
instrumental, synergy, "proven track record", "cross-functional collaboration", "fast-paced
environment", "a variety of", "responsible for".

Plain verbs read as human: built, designed, ran, shipped, wrote, cut, led, tested, moved, fixed,
launched, grew, reduced.

## Numbers that read as made up

- Suspiciously round or huge with no baseline: "increased engagement by 300%".
- Every bullet with a percentage, all of them improvements.
- A number the person can't explain in two sentences.

Real numbers are specific and sometimes modest: "cut checkout from 5 steps to 3", "tested with 12
users", "shipped to 4 markets in 6 weeks".

## File-level tells

- PDF metadata naming a generator (`/Producer` or `/Creator` mentioning an AI tool or a headless
  renderer). Strip it.
- Odd characters from copy-paste: smart quotes mixed with straight, non-breaking spaces, stray
  bullet glyphs.
- First person (I, me, my) in bullets.
